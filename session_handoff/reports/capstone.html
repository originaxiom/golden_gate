3 · Successor III — the Selector Existence Theorem

Theorem (weak form — and this form is proved). Let T be any theory (a consistent, recursively axiomatized formal system) and let W ∈ {0,1}ℕ be a record such that W is not computable from T. Then T decides at most finitely many… in fact fails to decide infinitely many bits of W.

Proof. If T decided all but finitely many bits, a machine enumerating T's theorems would compute W from a finite patch — contradicting non-computability. ∎

Reading. Take C1 ontologically: the actual world's record never compresses into a closed theory — that is what "description is inexhaustible" says of reality rather than of the object. Then selections must exist and must recur forever: infinitely many bits of actuality that no theory — this programme's or any other — derives. Their existence is forced by the axiom; their values are free by the same axiom; the object's forced content is exactly the T-decidable part. The gaps in the ledger are not unfinished work: they are the theorem's conclusion.

The strong form, and its honest gap. One wants more: that the undecided bits are algorithmically random relative to T (Chaitin-style), which would make the uniform priors of §1 not merely canonical but forced. That needs C1 formalized as an incompressibility bound K(W↾n) ≥ f(n) with f unbounded and a calibration of f — a genuine open problem in the formalization, named here rather than blurred. The Sturmian instance shows the subtlety: a golden word with computable phase is itself computable, so the hypothesis is about the record of closings, not about any one mathematical object.

10 · The completed picture

Not-nothing has two halves: why is there structure, and why is there this.

The axiom — reality forced from the minimal description that cannot close — answers both, asymmetrically. It answers the first by forcing: the golden selection, the E₆ entrance, the rung spectrum, the walls, the dial — everything this session verified, and it verified essentially all of it. It answers the second by proving the answer free: a minimal description cannot contain a basepoint, since a basepoint is more description; so every distinguishing choice — hand, arrow, phase, row, unit — is supplied as a perfect torsor and withheld as a point. The Selector Existence Theorem makes the withholding a corollary rather than a defect; the phase-blindness computation exhibits it in the programme's very first object; the measure table is what the object offers in place of the choice. The forced half is the theory. The free half is the event. The boundary between them — drawn this week in theorems, no-gos, and one long table of PASS lines — is the completed picture: not a theory that failed to reach the last ingredient, but a theory that reached, and proved the shape of, its own edge.
